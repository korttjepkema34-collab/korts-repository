Four pillars, one game

Each reference brings one system. The trick is that they share a clock: the day/night cycle is what makes hordes, building, bosses and cosy scavenging belong in the same game instead of four games.

From Survivor.io

The Wired come at night

Waves in the hundreds, simple steering, one screen of threat. Relics picked up during the night grant upgrades that last until dawn. The wall you built by day, and the door you did or did not fix, decide whether you survive it.

From Stardew Valley

The look, the movement, the daylight loop

Three-quarter top-down, eight-direction movement, 32×32 tiles, 32×48 characters, dense hand-crafted tile detail with 2D lighting on top. Walk behind walls and trees and they hide you. Walk into a house and the roof fades. Days are for scavenging, crafting, tending your Hold and talking to the few people left in the Keep.

From Elden Ring

Bosses, stamina, death that costs

Telegraphed patterns, dodge roll, stamina on every swing. Die and your unbanked relics drop where you fell; get back to them or lose them. Hearths, the working power nodes, are where you rest and bank.

From Minecraft

Your Hold

A tile plot inside the Keep you wall, roof, door and wire with scavenged material. Persistent across sessions and visible to every other player. Walls and doors take horde damage and need repair; a broken door at night lets the Wired inside.

The clock

DAY · 12 min
  • Scavenge the Fallows for scrap, food, relic fragments
  • Build and repair your Hold; wire relic power to lamps and turrets
  • Trade, take contracts from Hesper the Reeve, follow the campaign
  • Enter a dungeon: bosses live in day-time instances
NIGHT · 6 min
  • The Wired move on any light. Five waves, count scales with party size
  • Relics dropped by the horde grant night-long upgrades
  • The Keep's wall holds if the shared lamps are powered; a broken door lets them in
  • Dawn: unbanked relics bank. Dead players respawn at the keep

2.5D, and which kind

Your two references are two different techniques. Dragon Quest HD-2D puts pixel sprites inside a real 3D scene and lights it with bloom and depth-of-field blur. The Godot town is pure 2D pixel art at a large tile size with a three-quarter perspective and dense detail. What makes both feel alive is the same list: bigger tiles with more pixels each, lamps and windows that cast real light, shadows under everything, smoke and dust that move, and a soft blur at the top and bottom of the frame.

Recommendation: get there in 2D. Godot's 2D lights with normal-mapped sprites, particles, a day/night tint and one post-process shader for glow and edge blur produce the HD-2D feel without a 3D scene. Every tool in the studio stays the same; the tiles just get bigger and the reviewer gets stricter. True HD-2D with 3D geometry remains open later, since the same sprites and tiles become its textures. The mock frames on this page are built that way: 16-colour pixels underneath, a lighting pass on top.

Movement and the world you walk through

Three-quarter top-down, like the reference town. You move in eight directions with four-direction sprites. Everything is sorted by its feet, so walking behind a wall, a fence or a tree hides you behind it. Our twist: buildings are part of the same map. Walk through a door and the roof fades to show the room. Doors open, close, lock, and break. At night, a broken door is an invitation.

The world

The near future, forty years after the grid died. Nobody fixed it. What grew back was feudal: whoever holds a working reactor is a lord, whoever holds a printer is a priest, and everyone else farms, fights or scavenges. The old tech is not understood any more. It is venerated. A drone battery is a relic. A server rack is a shrine.

The Wired are the grid's last users. When it fell, the implant network kept running on people instead of servers. They are still linked, still walking, still looking for signal. They are drawn to light and power. Nobody knows if anyone is still in there.

Reapers are the outcasts who go into the dead fields to harvest relics from the Tithed and the ruins, because lords will pay in food and shelter for what a Reaper brings back. You are one. The lords do not let you inside their walls. So Reapers built their own, and called it the Keep.

The first quest · "Keep the Lights On"

The Keep's lamps are failing. Hesper knows there is a reactor in the Undercroft and knows who is standing on it. Get through the Fallows, get past the Castellan, bring the reactor home and wire it to the wall. The night after it goes in, the Wired come anyway, and they come toward the light. End of prototype.

Naming and text rules

The rule: medieval words that happen to describe the tech. Places are what they were for (the Keep, the Fallows, the Undercroft). Enemies are what the network made of people (Thrall, Courser, Herald, Castellan). Roles are old offices (Reeve, Reaper). Relics are old tech named as if holy (the Reactor, a Saint, the Rack). People get one short name and maybe a nickname (Hesper, Old Cutter). Dialogue: short, no exclamation marks, nobody explains the lore. UI: lowercase, terse. No elves, orcs, magic, purple glow or neon streets. Tech glows only when it works, and almost nothing works.
